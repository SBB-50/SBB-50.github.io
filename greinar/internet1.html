Curating a better internet experience.

As of right now, I am hopelessly addicted to my phone. It really can’t be helped honestly, because I like using the internet. The problem is not that I use the internet, but rather that I allow the internet to eat into my free time in a way that feels bad. I, like many others, blame this on short form video content, which seems to creep into every facet of my online existence, in a very intrusive manner. There is no popular social media website that is completely devoid of it, except for tumblr, but I don’t wanna use tumblr.

That said, I still quite like social media. Say what you will about the negative effects, it is still very nice to have some sort of distraction from the real world available at all times. I just don’t want it to be so consuming and depressing like it is now. I never feel great after watching tiktok for an hour, even if I did get a few interesting videos in between the clips of whatever shitcom is currently trendy and the rest of the slop I get on there. I would have deleted it a long time ago if i didn’t really like sending some bullshit to my friends every once in a while, even if the time it takes to find said bullshit only ever gets longer. The reason I don’t migrate to some other short form scroller that is „less addictive“ is that I simply don’t believe it. Everyone i know who has done so either gets just as addicted to the alternative (which is always instagram reels) and then just ends up reinstalling tiktok. The times I have done this have always ended up the same, where I scroll just as much but the videos are somehow even worse.

Recently however, I have made some improvements in how I use social media, at least to some extent. While I may not be free from the grips of short form video, I still feel like I’m getting closer to a form of internet usage which feels right. The main change I made is in how I access the internet. Instead of always using my phone (which I still do use a lot) I now also use my laptop. The obvious benefit of this is that vertical short form video isn’t made for computers. Both in the sense that you’re meant to be watching it in an app, rather than a website, but it’s also just meant for a different type of screen. Most of it is vertical, meaning it doesn’t take up the entire screen like it does on a phone. Something about this makes it much less of a struggle to stop scrolling it, if I even get started in the first place. The other (and main imo) benefit of this is that it makes my internet usage much more intentional. To access the internet I have to log into my computer, open up a browser and type in the name of the website I want to vist. This feels much better than the ingrained muscle memory of opening an app on my phone.

The thing with using a laptop is that it opens up more possibilities for internet usage. On my phone, all I ever do is go between two or three different apps, even when those become boring. Theres simply not much to really do, even if you technically can do way more. On my laptop, I can comfortably read articles and webcomics, play games like chess and go, or explore the indie web. These things are all technically possible on my phone, but in reality I never do them, because It’s just not as nice. The app based mobile experience really only lends itself to using the few main platforms that everyone uses, and all of them desperately try to keep you there, making fluid exploration of the internet almost impossible. Of course It’s nice to have a curated feed so that you don’t have to go looking for content, but having my internet usage be exclusively that just feels wrong, especially when it isn’t curated by me.
